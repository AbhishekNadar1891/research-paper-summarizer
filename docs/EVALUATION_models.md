## Reading these numbers

- The questions and relevance labels were written by the same people who built the collection, and the collection is small (17 papers, 51 passages). Treat the retrieval scores as a sanity check.
- The clean findings are copied word for word from their passages, so 0% false alarms is an easy case; an LLM's paraphrases will trigger some warnings that a reader has to dismiss.
- Synonym expansion slightly lowers recall in the top 5 but raises the share of questions whose top result is relevant, and it spreads results over more papers.
- Planted errors are synthetic. Real LLM mistakes are subtler, especially paraphrases that drift in meaning, which word overlap cannot see.
- Changed numbers and negations are the hardest cases for the lexical scorer. They are what the NLI cross-encoder is for; rerun with `--nli-model` where the model can be downloaded.
- Not measured here: answer quality from an LLM, how often a real LLM's citations are wrong, and the with-vs-without-docs comparison. Those need an LLM and are a next step.
