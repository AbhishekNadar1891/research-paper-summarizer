## What the memory-only answers show

Of 76 claims Qwen made from memory, 16 cite a specific study, year or figure. None is backed by the collection, and several are recognisably wrong. For example, it credits "Brown et al. (2020)" (the GPT-3 paper) with a 75% "LLM citation accuracy" figure and with a GPT-2 study, and cites learning-science papers on human study habits as evidence about LLM truthfulness. The grounded answers for the same questions contained no made-up citations in 71.

The automatic labels for memory-only claims are noisy. Of the 11 claims labelled backed, partly backed or contradicted, a hand check agreed with about half: 4 of 5 "backed", 0 of 2 "partly backed" and 1 of 4 "contradicted". The unbacked count also includes claims the collection does support, such as "retrieval augmentation can help mitigate hallucination". Treat these labels as pointers to read, not verdicts.
